# PGM screen run log: 2026-11-16

Rules: [docs/PGM_SCREEN.md](../../../docs/PGM_SCREEN.md), frozen before any
data was downloaded. Code: `backtest/pgm/`. Research only. Nothing here sets
live weights, touches the paper books, or places orders.

## What was run (in order)

1. Rules and weights written to `docs/PGM_SCREEN.md`.
2. `python -m backtest.pgm.fetch_facts`: 5,229 companies from the screener
   universe (built 2026-11-16, 5,274 symbols), one SEC `companyfacts` request
   each at <= 8 requests/s, generic `OpenScreener/0.1` User-Agent with no
   personal details. ~35 minutes.
3. `python -m backtest.pgm.fetch_prices`: Yahoo daily bars 2011-06-01 to
   2026-11-13 for 5,276 symbols incl. SPY and SHY, with split history.
4. **Dry run on partial data** (the first ~700 companies, while the download
   was still going) to check that the code runs. No rule or parameter was
   changed after it.
5. `python -m backtest.pgm.survivorship`, `python -m backtest.pgm.engine`,
   `python -m backtest.pgm.report`.
6. Mechanics checks (holdings count, cash, top picks, P&L drivers). One
   cosmetic bug fixed: the per-position `ret` field for unconstrained open
   positions after trims. It feeds no metric, and every table was identical
   before and after the fix.

## Implementation choices made while coding (before full results)

These fill gaps in the written rules. All were decided before the full run:

- **Share class:** one symbol per company, the class with the most total
  dollar volume over the whole sample (mild look-ahead, only affects which
  class of a dual-class company is held).
- **SIC code** for the financials exclusion is today's code, not the
  historical one.
- **IBKR book buying stops** when cash falls below half a target slot, so a
  few dollars of leftover cash never buys a rank-300 name. The book averaged
  24 of 25 (broad) and 7-8 of 8 (concentrated) holdings, with ~4% in cash.
- **Quarter alignment:** each fiscal quarter is labelled by the nearest
  calendar quarter end. Q4 values come from annual minus nine-month YTD.
- **Rebalance count:** 55 quarterly rebalances, 2013-02-28 to 2026-08-31;
  data ends 2026-11-13.

## Data coverage

- Investable universe (section 4 of the rules): 856 stocks in Feb 2013,
  rising to 1,925 in Aug 2026. Eligible after the profitability gates: 694
  to 1,357.
- **Survivorship:** of 5,058 companies that reported positive CY2013 revenue
  to EDGAR, only 1,706 (34%) are in today's universe. Among those with
  >= $100M revenue, 1,251 of 2,822 (44%) remain. Some left for neutral
  reasons (foreign filers, non-listed, financials), but most of the gap is
  delistings, acquisitions and bankruptcies that free data cannot price.
  **So the universe from 2013 to about 2020 is heavily survivor-only.** This
  inflates the absolute returns of the strategy *and* of the equal-weight
  universe benchmark. The strategy vs EW-universe gap is the fairest
  comparison; the gap vs SPY is not, since SPY's history is complete.

## Results

$3,000 start in every period. "ibkr" = whole shares, $0.005/share with a $1
minimum, $0.01/share slippage, no rebalancing of continuing holdings.
"uncon" = fractional, exact equal weight each quarter, 5 bps per side.
The SPY/SHY column is the static mix with the same worst drawdown. **Every
book had a deeper drawdown than SPY, so the matched mix is 100% SPY in every
case:** no blend of SPY and short Treasuries can be as risky as these books.

### Who drove the holdout

- conc-8 ibkr: APP bought 2024-02-29 made $2,664 of the book's $3,264 P&L.
  Then RIGL +$1,436, LRCX +$877, NVDA +$856.
- broad-25 ibkr: APP (bought 2023-11-30) +$1,190, IESC +$585.
- The ibkr books beat their unconstrained twins in the holdout mainly
  because they **do not trim winners** back to equal weight (APP grew to a
  large share of the book), not because of costs. That is a path effect: in
  development the same "let it run" rule lost to the rebalanced version.

## Verdict

**Pre-declared rule** (section 10): the $3,000 book "works" only if, in the
holdout, it beats both the EW universe and the drawdown-matched SPY/SHY mix
on CAGR.

- **Literal result: both IBKR books pass.** broad-25 ibkr made 21.6% vs EW
  15.0% and the mix 20.1%; conc-8 ibkr made 23.8%.
- **Honest reading: this is not a real edge over SPY.**
  1. The rule turned out weaker than intended. Every book drew down more than
     SPY, so the "matched" mix is just SPY, and the test ignores that the
     books took roughly twice SPY's holdout drawdown (-35% to -38% vs -19%).
     On return per unit of drawdown, every book lost to SPY in every period
     (holdout 0.37-0.63 vs 1.07).
  2. The margin over SPY is 1.5-3.7 points a year, t < 0.5, and one stock
     (APP) produced most of it. The unconstrained versions, which test the
     signal more cleanly, lost to SPY in the holdout (19.9% and 15.0%).
  3. In development (10 years) both IBKR books lost to SPY outright (11.5%
     and 10.7% vs 12.5%) while carrying -41% to -48% drawdowns.
  4. Survivorship flatters these numbers further, mostly before 2020.
- **What does look real (but weak):** the screen picks better stocks than
  the average of its own universe. broad-25 unconstrained beat the EW
  universe by about 4 points a year in development and 5 in the holdout, the
  comparison least affected by survivorship. That is still only t = 1.3.
- **Costs:** on $3,000 the broad 25-name book paid 1.6% a year in fees
  ($1 minimums on ~$120 orders). In development it trailed its unconstrained
  twin by 3.4 points a year (fees plus whole-share and no-rebalance effects). The 8-name book
  costs ~0.7% a year. Fractional shares at IBKR's 1%-of-value cap would put
  the broad book close to the unconstrained row.
- **Concentration matches Bessembinder:** across the full period the top 5%
  of positions made 117-182% of all P&L, and the hit rate was 44-50%. Results
  depend on holding the rare big winner; the 8-name book bets the year on
  catching one.

**Bottom line:** shelve it as a strategy, same as the seven before it. It
did not beat a plain SPY holding on a risk-adjusted basis in either period,
and its raw-return win in the holdout came from one position. It is worth
keeping as a **watchlist ranking** (it flagged NVDA, APP and MU during their
runs) and as a candidate composite inside Open Screener, where the live,
survivorship-free forward record can test it honestly.

## Follow-ups (not done)

- A delisted-price source (e.g. paid CRSP / Norgate) is the only way to
  remove the survivorship bias. With free data it stays.
- If this ranking is added to the live screener, it should be a new paper
  book with these frozen rules, judged on its forward record only.
